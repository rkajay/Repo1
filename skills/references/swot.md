# SWOT — Reference

Used to assess a *specific company or product* within the industry context established by Five Forces/PESTEL. SWOT without that context tends to be generic; always do Five Forces (at least briefly) first when the user wants SWOT for a company entering or competing in a defined market.

## The four quadrants

- **Strengths** (internal, positive): resources, capabilities, brand, IP, cost position, talent — things the company controls that give it advantage. Should be specific and evidenced (market share numbers, proprietary tech, customer retention data), not generic ("strong brand").
- **Weaknesses** (internal, negative): resource gaps, cost disadvantages, brand/reputation issues, capability gaps, dependency risks.
- **Opportunities** (external, positive): trends or gaps identified in the Five Forces/PESTEL analysis that this company is positioned to exploit — e.g. a weak/fragmented competitor set, a substitute threat that this company's product addresses, an emerging buyer segment.
- **Threats** (external, negative): trends or forces from Five Forces/PESTEL that could hurt this company specifically — e.g. low entry barriers inviting new competitors into its segment, a key supplier consolidating.

## Research approach
Search for the company's specific financials/market position (revenue, market share, recent product launches, customer reviews/NPS if available, recent leadership or strategy changes) rather than relying on brand reputation alone.

## Synthesis guidance
The most useful output is the **SO/WO/ST/WT cross**: how should strengths be used to capture opportunities, how should weaknesses be shored up against threats, etc. A plain four-quadrant list without this cross-analysis is the most common shallow version of SWOT — push past it to at least 2-3 concrete strategic implications.

## Common pitfalls to avoid
- Don't restate Five Forces findings as if they were internal (a high-buyer-power industry is an industry-level Threat for everyone, not a company-specific Weakness, unless this company is unusually exposed to it).
- Avoid vague entries ("good team," "competitive market") — every entry should be falsifiable with evidence.

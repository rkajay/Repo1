# PESTEL — Reference

Macro-environment scan, complementary to Five Forces. Used to surface external forces that shift the whole industry structure over time (Five Forces is more of a snapshot of current competitive structure; PESTEL looks at the broader forces that can move those structural factors).

## The six factors

- **Political**: government stability, trade policy, tariffs, taxation policy, government contracts/subsidies relevant to the industry.
- **Economic**: growth rates, interest rates, exchange rates, inflation, unemployment, consumer disposable income trends relevant to demand.
- **Social**: demographic shifts, cultural trends, consumer attitudes/behavior changes, health/lifestyle trends.
- **Technological**: rate of technological change, automation, R&D activity, disruptive technologies on the horizon.
- **Environmental**: climate/sustainability regulation, resource scarcity, ESG pressure from investors or consumers.
- **Legal**: industry-specific regulation, employment law, consumer protection law, antitrust/competition law, data protection law.

## Research approach
For each factor, search for recent (last 1-2 years) developments specific to the industry/geography in question rather than generic country-level facts. The goal is forward-looking: which of these factors is *actively changing* in ways that will reshape the Five Forces analysis over the next few years.

## Synthesis guidance
Don't treat all six as equally relevant — most industries have 2-3 PESTEL factors doing most of the work (e.g. tech-heavy industries are dominated by Technological + Legal/regulatory; commodity industries often dominated by Economic + Environmental). Identify which, and connect explicitly back to Five Forces: e.g. "tightening data privacy regulation (Legal) raises compliance costs that function as a new barrier to entry, reducing entrant threat but also raising costs industry-wide."
